Small Group Bible Study

In the Midst of the Storm

Thanksgiving Sunday · November 22, 2026
Text Acts 27:30–44 (ESV) · See also Acts 27:21–26
1Opening Up

As we celebrate Thanksgiving Sunday and look back on this past year, is there a person or a moment that makes you say, “I made it through because of them”?

2Reading the Passage

Take turns reading Acts 27:21–44 aloud, a few verses each. Then read verses 30–37 together once more, and notice the choices made on the same ship by the sailors and Paul, the soldiers and the centurion.

  • 30–32The sailors lower the ship’s boat to save themselves; “Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved”; the soldiers cut away the ropes
  • 33–38Paul urges the 276, starving for fourteen days, to eat; he takes bread, gives thanks, and breaks it; the rest of the wheat is thrown into the sea
  • 39–41At daybreak a beach comes into view, but the ship runs aground on a reef where two seas meet
  • 42–44The soldiers plan to kill the prisoners; the centurion, wishing to save Paul, stops them; “all were brought safely to land”
3Message Summary

The Lord is the captain of our lives

This ship headed for Rome was the Roman Empire in miniature. The centurion and soldiers carried the emperor’s authority, and the massive ship, its crew, and its hold full of grain symbolized the empire’s power and prosperity. They sailed in a dangerous season because shipping fees had risen to several times the usual rate. Gambling your life whenever there’s money to be made is the way of the world. At first a gentle south wind blew, and they boasted, “We made the right call.” But “not long after,” the northeaster struck. Gain we chase by ignoring God’s Word and following our desires doesn’t last long. That storm was God’s declaration: “Now I must become the Lord of your life.” To survive, they threw overboard even the cargo and grain that were the ship’s very reason for existing, yet still there was no hope of being saved. When the “cargo” we clutch is more precious to us than God, there is no hope of rescue.

On the fourteenth night, the sailors quietly lowered the ship’s boat to save only themselves. “Whoever’s meant to make it will make it” is the way of the world. But after hearing Paul’s warning, the soldiers cut away the ropes. We are a community bound by a common fate, joined together to live or die as one. The moment we forget that what happens to others happens to us, there is no way of salvation. Before 276 starving people, Paul took bread, gave thanks to God, broke it, and began to eat, and they were all encouraged and ate together. When land came into view, the soldiers brought back rank and status and tried to kill the prisoners, but God stopped them through the centurion, and all 276 were brought safely to land. They landed on Malta, just south of the Italian mainland. With no rudder and no oars, they had simply drifted, yet they arrived exactly where the Lord intended. Even when the course of our lives changes, it’s okay. The risen Lord is our captain.

“Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, ‘Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved.’”
Acts 27:31
4Observation Questions
  1. Why did the sailors lower the ship’s boat? What did Paul warn, and how did the soldiers respond? Acts 27:30–32
  2. In front of people who had gone fourteen days without food, what did Paul do and say? What changed in the people who watched him? Acts 27:33–38
  3. When land came into view, what did the soldiers and the centurion each choose? How was God’s promise to Paul finally fulfilled? Acts 27:24, 42–44
5Life Application
  1. Is there “cargo” in your life that you feel you can’t live without, yet it’s actually pulling you under? What might the Lord be asking you to let go of?
  2. Are you holding on to a “ship’s boat,” thinking, “As long as my family, my kids, and I are safe, that’s enough”? What would it look like for our group and our church to live as a community bound by a common fate?
  3. Who held you up during a stormy season this year? How could you express your thanks to them this Thanksgiving?
6Praying Together
  • That we would lay down the cargo of money, approval, and stubbornness we relied on more than the Lord, and take another step following His Word
  • That we would cut the ropes of the boat we kept ready to save ourselves, and never let go of one another’s hands as a community that weeps and rejoices together
  • That members in the middle of storms of illness, financial hardship, family pain, or anxiety about the future would experience the Lord’s comfort
  • That we would give thanks to the Lord, our captain, who has led us this far, and become a church overflowing with words of gratitude
7Closing Prayer

Lord, as we celebrate Thanksgiving Sunday, we look back on this past year. There were days when the south wind blew gently, but there were also stormy seasons when we couldn’t even tell where we were headed. There were days when our plans collapsed and the things we trusted could no longer protect us, and we were afraid and lost. Yet we confess that we are worshiping here together today not because our skill and experience were enough, but because the risen Lord has been the captain of our lives. Have mercy on us, for even as we said we could not live without You, our hands would not let go of money, approval, and our own stubbornness. Help us lay down the things we relied on more than You. Help us cut the ropes of the boat we kept ready to save ourselves, and the attitude that says it’s enough if only my family and my children are doing well. Help us never forget that we are one body in You, a church called to weep together and rejoice together. Remember those who are still in the storm, and comfort those worn down by illness, financial hardship, family pain, and anxiety about the future. Keep us from covering their pain with hasty words, and make us a church that stays beside them, prays, and truly shares their burdens. Not every problem has been solved yet, but we thank You for never leaving us alone. We haven’t received everything we hoped for, but we thank You for giving us a family of faith to walk with. Though we cannot know every part of the voyage ahead, help us follow Your Word, and keep us from letting go of one another’s hands along the way. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ, our life and our hope.

Amen.
Leader’s Notes

The Ship’s Boat and the Sailors’ Plan (27:27–32)

On the fourteenth night, the ship was being driven across the Adriatic Sea (v. 27). Taking soundings, the sailors found the depth dropping from twenty fathoms to fifteen and knew land was near (v. 28). The ship’s boat was a small dinghy towed behind or carried on board, used for ferrying cargo or towing; they had already hoisted it aboard in verse 16. Pretending to lay out anchors from the bow, the sailors lowered it into the sea (v. 30). Since they were the only people who knew how to handle the ship, their escape would have been disastrous for everyone left behind.

Took Bread, Gave Thanks, and Broke It (27:35)

The wording “took bread… giving thanks… broke it” echoes Luke’s accounts of feeding the five thousand (Luke 9:16), the Last Supper (Luke 22:19), and the table at Emmaus (Luke 24:30). That is why the sermon calls it “communion on the ship.” Since most people on board were not believers, it is best understood not as the church’s sacrament itself, but as a meal of thanksgiving to God that shared life with everyone.

Why the Soldiers Wanted to Kill the Prisoners (27:42)

Under Roman practice, a soldier who let a prisoner escape could be made to bear that prisoner’s penalty. Herod’s execution of the guards after Peter’s escape (Acts 12:19) and the Philippian jailer’s attempt to take his own life (Acts 16:27) show this background.

Malta (27:44; 28:1)

The island they reached was Malta, south of Sicily and just below the Italian peninsula. A ship that had only meant to go the short distance from Fair Havens to Phoenix ended up near Rome. The promise of verse 26, “we must run aground on some island,” came true exactly. Paul and his companions stayed there three months before sailing on to Rome (28:11).

Facilitation Tips

  • Suggested timing: Opening up 10 min · Reading and summary 15 min · Observation 15 min · Application 20 min · Prayer 15 min
  • Since it is Thanksgiving Sunday, give plenty of time for gratitude in Opening Up and Application 3.
  • If someone in your group is in the middle of a storm right now, listen first rather than offering quick words like “It’ll all work out,” and look for concrete ways to share the burden together. This is also the emphasis of the sermon and the closing prayer.
  • Application 2 is not a time to judge one another but to examine our own “ship’s boat.” It helps if the leader shares honestly first.
  • As you close, invite each person to say to someone in the group, “I made it through because of you.”
