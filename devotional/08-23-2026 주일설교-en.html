DAY 01 · Discernment

Even Praise
Must Be
Discerned.

Extraordinary ability and popular approval are never proof, by themselves, that God is at work.

"These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to you the way of salvation."

Acts 16:17 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

Everything the spirit said
was technically true.

On their way to the place of prayer, Paul and his companions were followed by a slave girl with a spirit of divination, who cried out, "These men are servants of the Most High God." Every word of it was accurate. And yet Paul was greatly troubled.

A spirit of deception has no interest in truth. Impressive gifts and remarkable results are never, by themselves, proof that God is at work. People will gather eagerly for their own reasons, even when Jesus is not the reason at all.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Take a second look at whatever looks impressive today.

01 When things go well even without prayer, do I simply celebrate — or does anything in me grow uneasy?
02 Where in my life or church have I mistaken being admired for being faithful?

Lord, keep me from mistaking what is dazzling for what is true.
When success comes without you, keep me awake, not comfortable.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · Faith as Muscle Memory

The Muscle
Memory of
a Praying Life.

Beaten and thrown into the darkest cell, what came out of them was prayer and praise.

"About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them."

Acts 16:25 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

It was not a prayer meant to be heard —
but the world heard it anyway.

A money dispute was dressed up as patriotism and disguised as prejudice against foreigners. Paul and Silas were severely flogged and thrown into the inner cell, their feet fastened in stocks. Even so, what rose from them was not complaint, but prayer and praise.

A daily, embodied faith can stand in worship in any circumstance. A believer's faith is revealed most plainly by how they respond in suffering.

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

Recall what came out of you in your hardest moment.

01 In your most painful recent moment, what actually came out of your mouth — complaint, or prayer?
02 If someone is "listening" to your response to hardship right now, what are they hearing?

Lord, give me a faith that is habit, not just circumstance.
Let praise rise in me even in chains, instead of complaint.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · A Different Instinct

More Frightening
Than Escape —
Staying.

The doors flew open and every chain came loose. Still, not one prisoner ran.

"But Paul cried with a loud voice, 'Do not harm yourself, for we are all here.'"

Acts 16:28 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

We need an entirely
different set of instincts.

A great earthquake shook the prison open and loosened every chain. The jailer, about to take his own life, trembled — not because the prisoners had fled, but because, though free to run, not one of them had.

Christians need instincts unlike the world's — the instinct not to rush to judgment about what is unseen or not yet revealed, and the instinct to see God's hidden work even when the visible reality is exhausting.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Recall a moment you could have walked away — but didn't.

01 Was there a moment you were free to leave but chose to stay? What made you stay?
02 What in your life right now convinces you that "what is seen is all there is"?

Lord, open my eyes to see beyond what is visible.
Let my staying be my witness to you.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Laying Down Rights

What It Costs
to Lay Down
a Right.

Jim Elliot had a gun and chose not to use it. Christ had power and chose not to use it.

"He emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant... he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross."

Philippians 2:7–8 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

Because they refused to use
their own power, a tribe was saved.

In 1956, missionary Jim Elliot and four companions were killed while seeking to reach the Waorani people of Ecuador. A pistol was later found in his pocket — he could have defended himself, and chose not to. His wife, Elisabeth, said, "If he had shot them, they would never have met Jesus."

Years later, through Elisabeth, the Waorani came to faith in Christ. Their chief testified, "Because of their deaths, we came to see the light." Christ, too, refused to use his rights on the cross — and because of it, we are saved today.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

Name one right you may need to lay down.

01 Is there a right you could rightfully claim, but should lay down for love's sake?
02 Where might laying down that right become a place someone sees Christ?

Lord, as you laid down your rights on the cross,
teach me to lay down mine for love.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · A Gospel Unchained

Paul Was Bound —
But the Gospel
Was Not.

There is a life that makes the world ask, "What must I do to be saved?"

"And they said, 'Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household.'"

Acts 16:31 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

Compromising the truth
has never saved the world.

Today's church often softens the truth so as not to unsettle the world. But the world has never turned back to God because we hid the truth that Jesus alone is Savior. Truth unsettles precisely so it can awaken us before it is too late.

The jailer asked, "What must I do to be saved?" He asked because he had witnessed people who saw beyond what could be seen. Paul was bound in that prison — but the gospel he carried was never bound at all.

Closing Prayer

One Thing to Carry
from This Week.

01 This week, where might your life lead someone to ask, "What must I do to be saved?"
02 Who is the one person you could tell the truth to — without compromising it, and still in love?

Lord, let me hold the truth without hiding it, and speak it in love.
Let us become people who see beyond what is seen.

— Amen.
