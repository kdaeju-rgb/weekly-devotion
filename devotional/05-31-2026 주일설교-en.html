DAY 01 · Grace That Stops Us

Being stopped
on the wrong path
is grace.

We are the only creatures that run faster when lost. The hand that stops that sprint is the greatest blessing we can receive.

"Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?"

Acts 9:4 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

Being stopped
on the wrong path
is grace.

Psychologist Rollo May once observed, "Human beings are the only animals who run faster when they are lost." Rather than admit a wrong turn, we press harder — hoping momentum will compensate for misdirection.

The greatest gift we can receive is someone who stops us while we are still running the wrong way. Today, that someone is the Lord himself, and his hand is reaching out.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Pause today. Examine the direction you've been running.

01 Have you lately asked whether what you're working so hard for is truly the direction the Lord desires?
02 Have there been moments when you ran faster just to avoid stopping? Where was God in those moments?

Lord,
I have grown so used to running
that I have forgotten how to stop.
Grant me grace today to be still and hear your voice.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · Misguided Zeal

What if my greatest
efforts have been
hurting you, Lord?

Saul was utterly convinced he was serving God. Yet that very zeal was an act of persecution against him.

"I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting."

Acts 9:5 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

Our zeal
can sometimes
wound the Lord.

Saul walked 135 miles to Damascus, certain that arresting followers of Jesus was God's will. What he believed was devoted service turned out to be persecution of God himself.

We must honestly ask: is my passion and dedication a mask for my own ambition? Could the Lord, in fact, be grieved by the very zeal I believe honors him?

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

Bring the motives behind your devotion honestly before God.

01 If you asked whether what you're pursuing is truly for the Lord or for yourself, what would your honest answer be?
02 Is there a misdirected ambition disguised as calling in your life? What would it take to lay it down before God?

Lord,
Let me honestly examine
whether my zeal is truly for you
or for myself.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · When Scales Fall Away

Sometimes
blindness
is where sight begins.

Only after three days of darkness did the scales fall from Saul's eyes — and new eyes, lifted upward, were opened.

"Something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight."

Acts 9:18 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

To regain sight
is to look
upward.

The Greek word ἀναβλέπω (anablepō) means both "to see again" and "to look up." Saul's restored sight was not merely physical — it was a spiritual awakening: the ability to behold the risen Christ.

Sometimes it takes illness, loss, or a broken season of life to make us finally look upward. Seasons of darkness can be the very gift of new eyes.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Reflect on what scales may still be covering your eyes.

01 Is there something you have seen but refused to acknowledge? What name would you give that scale?
02 Has a painful or dark season ever made you see God more clearly? What happened?

Lord,
Remove whatever covers my eyes.
Let me truly see the risen Christ
whom I have been looking past.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Nourished for Life

No longer
merely
surviving.

When Saul ate and was strengthened, it was a declaration: he now lives not to survive, but clothed in the power that comes from above.

"Taking food, he was strengthened."

Acts 9:19 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

Every meal, every morning,
every ordinary task —
clothed in God's strength.

The Greek λαμβάνω (lambano) means "to receive true life," and ἐνισχύω (enischyo) — "to be strengthened" — is the very same word used of the angel who strengthened Jesus in Gethsemane.

Saul was no longer merely keeping himself alive. He was now receiving strength from above. Every meal, every night's rest, every day's work became a moment of receiving divine power.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

See today's ordinary moments through the lens of true life.

01 Is there something you are doing just to get through it? What would it look like to invite God's strength into that very thing?
02 If the same strength that sustained Jesus in Gethsemane is promised to you, what prayer would you pray today?

Lord,
Let this not be a day of merely getting by —
but a day of eating for true life
and living clothed in your strength.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · Authentic Baptism

A clear line of faith
drawn through
every ordinary day.

We will not keep one arm out of the water. We die with Christ and rise with Christ — in eating, working, and living.

"He rose and was baptized."

Acts 9:18 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

Let every meal,
every morning,
be baptized in Christ's name.

When Constantine's soldiers were baptized, some held their sword arm out of the water — Christianity without the cross. Kanzo Uchimura, by contrast, baptized himself alone in the rain, and the old self was truly put to death and the new self raised.

We too have been baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. The call is not to trudge through work and family like the living dead, but to consecrate every meal, every morning, every ordinary task as holy service to God.

Closing Prayer

One clear line drawn this week —
a decision not to live as before.

01 Where in your life are you holding an arm out of the water — going through the motions while keeping something held back?
02 This week, if you were to baptize just one area of your ordinary life in Christ's name, what would it be?

Lord,
Let me die with Christ and live with Christ —
and be a true disciple
who lives for you alone.

— Amen.
