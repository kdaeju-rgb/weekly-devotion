DAY 01 · Unity Beyond Diversity

Gathering people together
does not, by itself,
make them one.

Different backgrounds, different colors, different cultures — how did they become one church?

"Now there were in the church at Antioch prophets and teachers, Barnabas, Simeon who was called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen a lifelong friend of Herod the tetrarch, and Saul."

Acts 13:1 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

What united the church at Antioch
was not what they had in common — it was Christ.

Barnabas from Cyprus, Simeon and Lucius from North Africa, Manaen who grew up beside King Herod himself, and Saul. Five leaders, each from a completely different race, background, and social standing.

Simply gathering people in one place does not make them one — if anything, it makes it easier to divide and draw harder lines. The one thing these five leaders held in common was Jesus Christ, and Christ alone.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Begin today by thinking of someone different from you.

01 When you meet someone whose background and temperament differ from yours, what do you notice first?
02 What is the one thing that could make you and that person truly one?

Lord,
keep me from drawing lines before those who differ from me.
Teach me what it means to be one in Christ alone.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · The Name "Christian"

A people who cannot
be explained
apart from Christ.

It may have begun as mockery, but the world had no other word for them.

"...and in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians."

Acts 11:26 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

The same gospel is heard
by two people — and still, they choose differently.

Herod Antipas and Manaen were raised together from childhood, nursed at the same breast, closest of friends. Both saw Jesus. Both heard the same gospel. Yet one conspired to kill him, and the other believed and gave his whole life to the very Jesus his friend had killed.

The name "Christian" was never a matter of pedigree or achievement — it belongs only to those who have given their lives to Christ. When what we know and how we live become one, the world has no choice but to call us by that name.

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

Ask honestly whether your name matches your life.

01 Am I living up to the name "Christian"?
02 Have there been moments when I responded like Manaen — or like Herod — to the very same word?

Lord,
I want my life to be inexplicable apart from you.
Make what I know and how I live one and the same.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · Fasting as Everyday Worship

Fasting was not
a special occasion —
it was their everyday worship.

Even while the church was thriving, its leaders kept fasting and depending on God.

"While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, 'Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.'"

Acts 13:2 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

To fast is to cut off
our own desires and our own sin.

Antioch was, by any measure, a thriving, sought-after church. Nothing was wrong. And yet its leaders fasted anyway. The original language places worship and fasting side by side, as one and the same act — "worshiping the Lord and fasting."

They humbly emptied their hearts before God, and the Holy Spirit himself filled that emptiness. Depending on God even when nothing is wrong — that is true worship.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Seek an empty heart even when life is going well.

01 Do I depend on God even when everything seems fine?
02 What one desire or sin do I need to cut off today?

Lord,
even when life is going well, keep me from relying on my own strength.
I come with an empty heart, asking to be filled by your Spirit.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Giving Our Presence

Mission begins
with giving away
our presence.

Mission is not only about sending missionaries overseas.

"Then after fasting and praying they laid their hands on them and sent them off."

Acts 13:3 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

Before we go and gather people in,
we must first become a sending people.

Letting go of Barnabas and Saul must have been a fearful, painful decision for the leaders of Antioch. It could easily have slowed their own growth. Still, they obeyed the voice of the Spirit.

Mission means giving our very presence to the place where we live, welcoming everyone in the name of the Lord. Every time believers step into their homes, workplaces, and schools, that place becomes a mission field.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

See where you are today as your mission field.

01 Which of your home, workplace, or school is your mission field today?
02 Is there something you have been holding onto that God may be asking you to release?

Lord,
help me see where I stand today as a mission field.
Give me the faith to let go and give freely, rather than hold on.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · A Church That Sends

Not a church
that gathers people in,
but one that sends them out.

Not a church that builds ever higher, but one that flows ever further.

"...While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting... Then after fasting and praying they laid their hands on them and sent them off."

Acts 13:1-3 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

Not a church that raises
higher walls, but one that opens a road.

Refusing to hold on to the people God had given them as their own possession, and releasing them back into God's larger purposes — that is the picture of a missional church that Antioch left us.

A healthy church is not measured only by numbers. It is measured by how well it raises, trains, and sends its people — and by the fact that wherever they go, God's kingdom work begins again.

Closing Prayer

This week,
live as one built on the road.

01 This week, who or what do you need to release rather than hold on to?
02 What single sentence from this week's devotional will you carry into your daily life?

Lord,
let our church not be a fortress with high walls,
but a road on which anyone may find rest in you.

— Amen.
