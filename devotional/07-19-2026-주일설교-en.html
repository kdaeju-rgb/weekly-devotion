Acts 12 · A Study in Prayer and Faith

Even without certainty,
clinging to God is faith.

One reading a day — a single verse, a key truth, a short meditation, and a prayer. Five days to walk together through the week.

DAY 01 · Loss We Cannot Understand

The happier we are,
the deeper the emptiness
can grow.

Like Levin in Tolstoy's novel, we too can feel that life loses its meaning in the face of death.

"About that time Herod the king laid violent hands on some who belonged to the church. He killed James the brother of John with the sword."

Acts 12:1-2 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

Even a life tossed here by chance
has been given a clear direction by God.

In Tolstoy's novel, Levin suddenly thinks of death on the very day his first child is born — the happiest day of his life. The happier he feels, the deeper his emptiness grows.

But a farmer he meets tells him, "Life is simply to be lived, and lived well." Today's misfortune can become tomorrow's joy — that is the hope we carry as those who believe God stands behind our days.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Look for God's direction even inside today's loss.

01 Is there a loss or pain right now that you simply cannot make sense of?
02 If you believed God stood behind that very time, how would today look different?

Lord,
even in the face of loss I cannot understand,
do not let me leave my life without meaning.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · Not God's Failure

Without the cross,
there is no glory
to speak of.

We can neither understand nor guess why James died so early.

"And when he had seized him, he put him in prison, delivering him over to four squads of soldiers to guard him."

Acts 12:4 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

Not being able to understand
does not mean God is wrong.

Herod Agrippa executed James to win favor with the Jewish people, then seized Peter as well, guarding him with sixteen soldiers around the clock.

We cannot know or guess why James was allowed to die so soon. Yet this is not God's failure — after all, this is the God who did not spare even his own Son from the cross. A gospel of glory without a cross is not God's word, but the devil's temptation.

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

Trust God even before suffering you cannot explain.

01 Is there someone near you enduring a suffering you cannot understand?
02 Where in your life might you need to be ready to pay a cost, rather than expect only blessing?

Lord,
even before suffering I cannot explain,
do not let me doubt your goodness.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · Praying Without Certainty

"You are
out of your mind" —
yet God had already answered.

Neither Rhoda nor the believers praying inside could believe the answer had come.

"So Peter was kept in prison, but earnest prayer for him was made to God by the church."

Acts 12:5 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

Even without certainty,
clinging to God is faith.

When Rhoda ran in shouting that Peter was at the door, the believers told her, "You are out of your mind." They had prayed all night, yet could not believe their own prayer had been answered.

A church in Kansas once gathered to pray for rain during a drought. Everyone shouted "Amen!" — yet no one had brought an umbrella. Even when certainty is thin, praying is still faith.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Cling to God in prayer today, even without certainty.

01 Is there something you have been putting off praying about because you lack certainty?
02 Like the early church who prayed without an umbrella, what will you ask God for today?

Lord,
even without certainty,
let me simply cling to you in prayer today.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Faith Fixed on a Person

Faith holds on
to a person,
not to an outcome.

Like Jacob wrestling through the night, faith is holding on to the one God alone.

"They kept saying, 'It is his angel!'"

Acts 12:15 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

Prayer is not securing my will —
it is surrendering control to God.

True faith is never fixed on the certainty of an outcome; it is always fixed on the person of God. Whether the door opens or not, we have no choice but to cling to him.

Prayer is not pleading for my will to be done, but asking that God's will be done. Our goal is not to make our own vessel of faith large and impressive, but to carry the precious Lord in however plain a jar we are.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

Ask for God's will today, not just your own.

01 Is there a prayer lately where you were really only asking for your own will to be done?
02 What changes if you hand the control of that prayer over to God?

Lord,
not my will, but yours —
let that be the prayer I pray.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · The Word Increased

Led halfway,
we walk the rest
by obedience.

The angel led Peter only to the edge of the city — the rest of the way was Peter's to walk.

"But the word of God increased and multiplied."

Acts 12:24 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

On the road we fill in
with obedience,
we meet God.

The angel led Peter out of the prison but then simply left him. Peter had to decide, on his own, where to go from there. God always leads us only halfway, and asks the rest of us in free, obedient faith.

A chapter that began with James's martyrdom and Peter's imprisonment ends with, "the word of God increased and multiplied." When we live by clinging to God without full certainty, the same ending is given to us.

Closing Prayer

This week, one thing
to cling to without certainty.

01 This week, what is the "other half" of the road you must walk by obedience?
02 What single sentence from this week's devotional has stayed with you the most?

Lord,
even when my heart is not full of certainty,
let me depend on you alone, today and always.

— Amen.
