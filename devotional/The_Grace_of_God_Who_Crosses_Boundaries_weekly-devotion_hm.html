Original Text

Title: The Grace of God Who Crosses Boundaries
Text: Acts 10:25-35, 44-47

Key movements:

  • Cornelius, a Gentile, feared God and responded through prayer and generosity.
  • Peter was taught again, through a vision, that God’s categories of clean/unclean were not to be used as human prejudice.
  • God does not show partiality, but receives those from every nation who fear Him and do what is right.
  • The Holy Spirit was poured out on Gentiles as well, and the church was called to rejoice in that grace.

Summary

This sermon centers on the truth that God pours out grace beyond the boundaries humans create.
Jewish and Gentile prejudice had become a strong cultural and religious wall, but God prepared both Cornelius and Peter to tear that wall down.

Peter’s confession becomes the key statement of the passage:

“God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears Him and does what is right is acceptable to Him.”

The gospel is not the property of one group; it is open to all whom God calls.

Insights

1) We often interpret others through our own assumptions

Without knowing the whole story, we easily judge.
Our judgment is often distorted by prejudice and incomplete knowledge.

2) God sees the heart, not the outward form

Cornelius’ prayer and generosity were not the basis of salvation, but they revealed a genuine response to God.
God remembered that response and brought him to the place of the gospel.

3) The gospel crosses borders

Peter entered a Gentile home, crossing religious and cultural lines.
This shows that the church is not meant to be a community that divides people, but one that carries grace outward.

4) The Holy Spirit is not bound by our sequence

The Spirit came while Peter was still speaking, not after he had finished.
Salvation always belongs to God’s initiative.

Action Items

Reflection questions for this week

  1. Who am I judging too quickly by my own standards?
  2. What boundary have I built that God may be asking me to cross?
  3. Am I overlooking someone whom God has already received?
  4. Do I rejoice in the grace others receive, or compare myself with them?

Practice for this week

  • Pray daily for a heart free from prejudice
  • Bless one person who makes you uncomfortable
  • Celebrate someone else’s spiritual growth with sincerity
  • Confess: “God loves me, and He also loves them.”

Short Prayer

Lord, help me believe that Your grace is wider than the boundaries I build.
Keep me from judging by appearance or background, and make me humble enough to follow the path where the gospel goes.
Give me a teachable spirit like Peter, and a God-fearing response like Cornelius. Amen.
