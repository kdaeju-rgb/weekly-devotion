DAY 01 · The Cost of Being Right

Being right
isn't enough.

The early church faced an identity crisis over the salvation of the Gentiles.

"But some men came down from Judea and were teaching the brothers, 'Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved.'"

Acts 15:1 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

Being right doesn't guarantee
you'll be heard rightly.

A pastor's, a teacher's, a parent's correction is rarely factually wrong. Point by point, it usually holds up. And yet however true it is, if it stings too much, it can make us want to do the opposite.

The early church faced something similar. That Gentiles — uncircumcised, unfamiliar with the law — could be saved by faith in Jesus alone was, to many Jewish believers, simply unthinkable.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Today, examine how you deliver what's right.

01 When you're convinced you're right, how have you tended to deliver it?
02 Has being right ever pushed someone further away instead of drawing them closer?

Lord,
give me the wisdom to speak what I believe is true
with love, not just conviction.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · Standing Firm in Love

Avoiding conflict
isn't the same as love.

Paul and Barnabas would not stay silent before a false gospel.

"And after Paul and Barnabas had no small dissension and debate with them, Paul and Barnabas and some of the others were appointed to go up to Jerusalem to the apostles and the elders about this question."

Acts 15:2 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

Smoothing things over
cannot protect the truth.

Those who came down from Judea argued, based on Genesis 17:12, that Gentiles must be circumcised to be saved. Paul and Barnabas could not agree.

They endured beatings and persecution for the sake of the gospel — but before a teaching that threatened to unravel the church, they stood firm. Pleasing everyone was never their goal; guarding the truth was.

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

This week, choose an honest word over silence.

01 Has there been a moment in your church community where you sensed something was wrong but stayed quiet?
02 Was "let's just keep the peace" ever really peace — or was it avoidance?

Lord,
don't let my fear of conflict silence the truth.
Give me courage to contend when contending is needed.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · The Answer Is in the Word

They searched Scripture,
not just their feelings.

Antioch didn't paper over the conflict — they sought sound theology.

"And God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us, and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith."

Acts 15:8-9 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

The salvation of the Gentiles
wasn't a sudden turn of events.

Peter testified that God gave the Holy Spirit to the Gentiles exactly as he had to Jewish believers, making no distinction between them. Paul and Barnabas reported the signs and wonders God had done among the nations.

James quoted Amos 9 to show that the restoration of "David's tent" had always meant a kingdom that included the Gentiles. It was God's plan from the beginning, not an afterthought.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Open Scripture before you open your opinion.

01 Have you brought a current struggle to the Word before you brought it to your feelings?
02 Is the foundation of your judgment emotion, or Scripture?

Lord,
when I face conflict and confusion,
let me open your Word before I open my mouth.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Guard the Core, Yield the Rest

Hold the core firmly.
Hold the rest loosely.

James affirmed Gentile salvation while still honoring Jewish believers.

"'All things are lawful,' but not all things are helpful. 'All things are lawful,' but not all things build up."

1 Corinthians 10:23 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

Not a condition for salvation —
an act of love.

James affirmed that Gentiles were saved by faith, yet he did not call for the law to be abolished altogether. He proposed a few minimal requirements — abstaining from food polluted by idols, from sexual immorality, and from what has been strangled and from blood.

These were meant to keep Jewish believers, who still read the law of Moses in synagogue every Sabbath, from being offended. Even a firm conviction needs the humility to consider the person receiving it.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

Consider one heart before you assert your conviction.

01 Among the things you're convinced you're right about, is there something you could yield out of love for someone else?
02 Between those who cherish tradition and those who long for change, whose heart do you need to understand better right now?

Lord,
don't let my conviction cause someone else to stumble.
Give me the wisdom to hold the essential and release the rest.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · Shared Story, Sound Theology

Shared experience
and sound theology.

A church needs both a story lived together and a faith built on the Word.

"So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."

Romans 10:17 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

Hold the essence of the gospel as absolute.
Hold everything else with an open hand.

Even a Spirit-filled early church was not free of conflict — the larger a church grows, the more conflict tends to grow with it. That's why a shared story, of weeping and praying through hard seasons together, matters so much.

At the same time, a church needs sound theology rooted in the Word. Without a clear theology of the church, ministry becomes a factory of programs. Worship and personal meditation on Scripture build that foundation.

Closing Prayer

Carry one person and one commitment into this week.

01 Who is one person you will weep and pray with this week?
02 What time of worship or meditation will you keep this week to build your foundation of faith?

Lord,
let me hold the essence of the gospel close to my heart,
and hold everything else with love, not rigidity.

— Amen.
