DAY 01 · A Love We All Need

We all need
to be loved.

We aren't loved because we're perfect. We rise again because we were loved.

"Above all, keep loving one another earnestly, since love covers a multitude of sins."

1 Peter 4:8 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

From the day her teacher first held her close,
Helen Keller's heart began to melt.

In a Boston asylum, a blind and broken girl named Anne Sullivan was restored through two years of relentless love from a nurse named Laura. The love she received, she later poured back into a child who could not see, hear, or speak.

Helen Keller once defined love this way: "The day my teacher came to me, she held me close." We are no different. However put-together we may appear, no one lives without wounds.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Recall this week the love that raised you back up.

01 Who has held you up when you couldn't stand on your own?
02 Are you becoming that kind of love for someone else right now?

Lord,
help me remember the love I have received,
and let me pour that same love out on someone today.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · A Departure Called Betrayal

Mark's leaving
was called betrayal.

Partway through the first missionary journey, Mark left Paul and Barnabas at Pamphylia.

"And the ones on the rock are those who, when they hear the word, receive it with joy. But these have no root; they believe for a while, and in time of testing fall away."

Luke 8:13 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

The word Paul used for Mark's "leaving"
was the very same word Scripture uses for "falling away."

Mark, the cousin of Barnabas, was chosen as an assistant for the first missionary journey — but as the team faced the Taurus Mountains, he turned back to Jerusalem. We can't know the exact reason. What is certain is that the resolve and faith he first carried gave way.

When the second journey came, Paul firmly refused to take Mark along, and he and Barnabas quarreled so sharply that they parted ways. Mark was left labeled unqualified.

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

Face the moment your life was branded a dropout.

01 Is there a moment you have judged yourself as having "left" or "given up"?
02 How has that failure gone on defining you since?

Lord,
don't let me only feel shame over where I fell —
let me bring it honestly before you.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · Restored as a Pillar

The one once called
a traitor became
a fellow worker.

Just before his martyrdom, the one person Paul most wanted to see again was Mark.

"Luke alone is with me. Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is very useful to me for ministry."

2 Timothy 4:11 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

The man once treated as a traitor,
Paul now called "my fellow worker."

Writing from a Roman prison, Paul records in Colossians that "Mark, the cousin of Barnabas" is with him. In Philemon, he calls Mark "my fellow worker." Peter, too, calls him "my son."

Mark went on to become one of the most important figures of the early church — the author of the Gospel of Mark. The marks of fear and failure were on him, but they were not his final story.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Believe that failure is not the final scene.

01 What story in your life have you assumed is already over?
02 What might it look like if God is still rewriting that story?

Lord,
let my failure not be the final sentence,
but only a single page in a story you are still writing.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Unconditional Love That Rebuilds

A supporter and a comforter
rebuilt Mark.

His mother Mary's unwavering prayers, and his cousin Barnabas's steady encouragement, stood behind him.

"And there arose a sharp disagreement, so that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus."

Acts 15:39 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

The name Barnabas itself
means "son of encouragement."

Mark's mother Mary was a wealthy widow who had remained faithful for ten years, risking everything to open her home as a place of prayer. When her only son came home having given up midway, she restored him with unconditional love and encouragement.

Even as he split from Paul, Barnabas took Mark and sailed to Cyprus to minister together. Judged unqualified by Paul, the young Mark kept growing steadily under Barnabas's encouragement and comfort.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

Become a Mary or a Barnabas for someone.

01 Do you have a Mary or a Barnabas standing beside you right now?
02 Who is waiting for you to become that person for them?

Lord,
make me someone who passes on the love I've received,
lifting up the fallen with encouragement, not condemnation.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · Turning the Scar into the Star

Getting up again
is what real faith
looks like.

Strong faith isn't never falling — it's rising again after we fall.

"And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to his purpose."

Romans 8:28 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

"Turning the scar into the star" —
a God who turns our wounds into light.

When a toddler takes three wobbling steps and falls, the whole family cheers. God, too, watches our clumsy steps of obedience and withholds no praise — because he knows that even after we fall, we will rise and take another step.

Just as Mark's failure and scars went on to encourage Paul, Peter, and us, the failures scored into our own lives will one day be written into something beautiful. Not by our own strength, but by his good power at work within us.

Closing Prayer

This week,
carry one thing from this devotional.

01 Where will you take a wobbling step of obedience this week?
02 How might you become a wounded healer for someone else?

Lord,
don't let me blame what I lack or where I'm weak;
let me take one wobbling step of obedience toward you today.

— Amen.
