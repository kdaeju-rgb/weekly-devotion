Small Group Bible Study

The Grace of Being Able to Hear

Sunday Worship · October 18, 2026
Text Acts 22:12–21 · See also Acts 9:10–19, 22:1–11, 22:22
1Opening Up

Has anyone ever asked you, "Are you even listening to me?" Share a time you heard something clearly but chose to tune it out, or a sound that always reaches you, no matter how noisy it is around you.

2Reading the Passage

Take turns reading Acts 22:12–21 aloud, a few verses each. Then read verse 22 together and notice the exact words at which the crowd stopped listening.

  • 12–13Ananias comes: "Brother Saul, receive your sight"
  • 14–16Called to be a witness of what he has seen and heard
  • 17–18Praying in the temple: "Get out of Jerusalem quickly"
  • 19–21Paul's hope and the Lord's answer: "to the Gentiles"
3Message Summary

God's Word Is Heard from Below

Facing an angry crowd, Paul starts by confessing his own shameful past: "I was zealous for God, just as all of you are." His zeal had been aimed in the wrong direction, and he had persecuted the believers. Then, on the Damascus road, he met the risen Lord and fell to the ground. "What shall I do, Lord?" he asked, and he handed control of his life back to Jesus. Blind, he was led into Damascus, and the first words Ananias spoke to him were, "Brother Saul, receive your sight." Ananias knew better than anyone who Saul had been, yet he did not let his own judgment run ahead of God's grace. Paul was then called to be a witness "for him." The point of his story would not be how much Paul had changed. It would be the Jesus who had met him.

Paul expected that the people of Jerusalem, who knew his past, would surely listen. But the Lord said, "Go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles." The crowd "listened up to this word" and then shut their ears (v. 22). The boundary they had drawn mattered more to them than the voice of God. In Greek, "to obey" (hypakouein) means "to hear from under," while "to disobey" (parakouein) means "to hear from beside." The same word can lead to obedience or disobedience, depending on where we stand as we hear it. Being able to hear is itself grace: the Lord came to us first and stopped us on our way. So our prayer today should not be "Lord, open that person's ears," but "Lord, keep my ears from closing."

"…came to me, and standing by me said to me, 'Brother Saul, receive your sight.' And at that very hour I received my sight and saw him."
Acts 22:13 (ESV)
4Observation Questions
  1. What were Ananias's first words to Saul? Considering what Ananias already knew about Saul, why do those words carry so much weight? Acts 22:12–13 · 9:13–14
  2. What mission did Ananias announce to Saul? What does the phrase "a witness for him" say about what should be at the center of a witness's life? Acts 22:14–16
  3. Why did Paul expect the people of Jerusalem to accept his testimony? How did the Lord answer, and at which words did the crowd stop listening? Acts 22:17–22
5Life Application
  1. Have you ever hurt someone in the name of "serving God" or "telling the truth"? Where in your life do you need to stop running on your own plan and ask, "What shall I do, Lord?"
  2. Is there someone you've written off because of their past ("that's just who they are")? Like Ananias, what could you say or do that expects God to work in that person? Please share without naming anyone.
  3. Do you hear comforting words "from below" but correcting words "from beside"? Is there a word from God you've been listening to only "up to this point" and no further?
6Let's Pray Together
  • That we would lay down misdirected zeal and daily ask, "What shall I do, Lord?"
  • That our group would not lock anyone in their past, but expect what God will do in them
  • That we would rejoice with God when His grace reaches the people we find hard to love
  • That we would entrust to the Lord the hearts of family and neighbors who won't listen, and keep our own ears open under His Word
7Closing Prayer

Father God, who speaks to us, You stopped Saul as he raced down the Damascus road, driven by his own zeal, and You spoke to him as he lay on the ground. May that same grace come to us today. We have heard Your Word many times, yet we have often listened from beside rather than from below. We welcome words that comfort us but close our ears to words that would correct us. Forgive our misguided zeal, which in Your name has wounded the very people You love. Give us the courage of Ananias, to go to the people we fear or find hard to love and call them "brother" and "sister." When our sincerity is not understood, keep us from losing heart. Help us to leave the opening of others' ears to You. "Speak, Lord, for Your servant is listening from below." In Jesus' name we pray.

Amen.
Leader's Notes

The Surrounding Context (22:1–11, 22)

After being seized in the temple, Paul stands on the steps under the protection of Roman soldiers and addresses the crowd in Hebrew. He calls them "Brothers and fathers," and tells them that he too, trained under Gamaliel, was zealous for God. Then he recounts his conversion on the Damascus road (vv. 1–11). The crowd listens quietly until the words "to the Gentiles," and then they erupt (v. 22). Today's passage (vv. 12–21) sits between these two moments: Ananias's ministry and Paul's commission.

Ananias (compare Acts 9:10–19)

In chapter 9, Ananias tells the Lord he hesitates: "I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he has done to your saints." Still, he obeys and greets him as "Brother Saul." In chapter 22, Paul describes him as "a devout man according to the law" (v. 12) to show his Jewish audience that this was a man they would respect.

The Death of Stephen (v. 20; Acts 7:58–8:1)

When Stephen was stoned, the witnesses laid their garments at Saul's feet, and Saul approved of his death. Because everyone in Jerusalem knew this, Paul expected his transformation to be a powerful testimony. Yet the outcome of his witness was not his to decide.

Hypakouein and Parakouein

"To obey" (hypakouein) combines hypo ("under") and akouein ("to hear"). "To disobey" (parakouein) combines para ("beside") and akouein. Paul heard the Lord's voice from below, lying face down on the road. The crowd listened from above, looking down, and only "up to this word." Bring this contrast back up during Application Question 3.

Facilitation Tips

  • Suggested timing: Opening 10 min · Reading & Summary 15 min · Observation 15 min · Application 20 min · Prayer 15 min
  • Application 2 is not about covering up wrongdoing or pressuring anyone who has been hurt to forgive. Accountability and safe boundaries still matter. The sermon's point is that our judgment must not run ahead of God's grace.
  • A testimony doesn't have to be dramatic. Encourage members that admitting a fault before God's Word, or praying for the first time for someone they resented, is a precious testimony.
  • If someone is discouraged because loved ones won't hear the gospel, pray together with the message that opening hearts is the Lord's work.
