Small Group Discussion Sheet

Debtor to the Gospel

Friday Young Adults Gathering · November 13, 2026
Text Romans 1:8–15 (ESV) · Reference Acts 18:2; Genesis 1:26–27; Romans 5:8 (ESV)
1Icebreaker

Have you ever been so grateful to someone that you felt you “owed” them? Is there someone you still feel you haven’t fully repaid? Who comes to mind?

2Message Summary

Repay the grace you’ve received to the person next to you

In AD 49, Emperor Claudius expelled all Jews from Rome, and the Roman church became a church made up entirely of Gentile believers. They held the church together and even saw it grow. But when Claudius died in AD 54 and the Jewish believers returned after about five years, a new conflict broke out: who would lead the ministry, Jewish pride, Gentile judgment, purity laws at the table, and differing views on salvation. None of this was for the sake of the gospel or for Jesus. Though all of them had been saved by grace alone, they forgot that grace and argued over who was better, saying, “Still, I’m better than you.” Yet before Paul addresses their faults, he first praises their faith and tells them he prays for them without ceasing, like a skilled doctor who sees not only the wound but the weight of the life behind it.

What made Paul, once a fierce man, so gentle? He confesses that he is “under obligation” to Greeks and barbarians, to the wise and the foolish. As one indebted to the Lord’s love, he believed that loving the people in front of him was how he repaid his debt to God. Every person bears the image of God: we reflect God like a mirror and represent God like an ambassador. So to sin against a person is to sin against God, and to love a person is to love God. Let’s not be like the servant who was forgiven ten thousand talents and then threw a friend into prison over twenty dollars. Whether the grace we received is real shows in how much grace we give to the one who hurt us most. A pillar standing alone is a ruin. It becomes a pillar only when it holds others up. Beat others and you come first; serve others and you become first-class. Paul himself was first a patient healed on the road to Damascus. And our debt is paid to the image of God sitting right next to us.

“I am under obligation both to Greeks and to barbarians, both to the wise and to the foolish.”
Romans 1:14
3Observation Questions
  1. Before pointing out the faults of the divided Roman church, what did Paul do first? Why does that order matter? Romans 1:8–9
  2. Paul never actually borrowed money from anyone. Why, then, does he say he is “under obligation” to Greeks and barbarians, the wise and the foolish? Romans 1:14; 5:8
  3. What are the two meanings of being made in the image of God (reflecting and representing)? How do they connect to the idea that “loving people is loving God”? Genesis 1:26–27
4Life Application
  1. Have you ever caught yourself thinking, “Well, at least I’m better than that person,” in our community? What grace had you forgotten in that moment?
  2. We can tell whether the grace we received is real by how much grace we show to the one who hurt us. If a “friend who owes you twenty dollars” comes to mind, what small step could you take this week?
  3. To become not the one who wins but the one who serves—a pillar who holds others up—whom could you build up this week in our group or around you, and how?
5Prayer Together
  • That we would never forget the love of Christ, who died for us while we were still sinners, and live humbly as debtors to grace
  • That instead of judging others’ faults, we would have Paul’s gentleness and humility to encourage and pray first
  • That we would see even those who hurt us as the image of God, and let the grace we’ve received flow to them
  • That even as the weather turns cold, we would become pillars who hold one another up and form a warm young adult community
6Closing Prayer

Loving Father God, thank You for showing Your love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Help us to see again the great grace that received us without condition, though we did not deserve it. Forgive our pride, when we forgot that grace and judged and condemned others over their small faults. Like Paul, let us live humbly as debtors to the gospel and debtors to grace. Open our eyes to see the brothers and sisters beside us as precious people made in Your image, and help us remember that loving those who endured hard days to come here is loving You. Let us live not to come first by beating others, but to be first-class by serving them, and make us pillars who hold others up and build them up. This week, let us carry Your love to everyone we meet with the heart of a debtor. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ, who saved us in love.

Amen.
