Small Group Discussion Sheet

But God…

Friday Young Adults Gathering · October 23, 2026
Text Genesis 50:15–21 (ESV) · Reference Genesis 41:51–52; 45:5; 48:17–19 (ESV)
1Icebreaker

As a child, did you ever hide a mistake and get caught (or finally confess)? How did your parents or the adults around you respond?

2Message Summary

We can’t change the past, but we can see it with new eyes

A little girl stained her parents’ new white sofa and carried the secret alone for a month. When it was discovered, instead of punishment she received a hug and the words “We love you.” The stain never came out, but it became evidence of love and forgiveness rather than a mark of shame. Another boy broke his father’s precious vase with a slingshot and buried the pieces. A servant who saw it blackmailed him, and the master’s son became the servant’s servant. Hidden sin enslaves us, but the moment we confess it to the Father, it loses its power. Joseph’s brothers carried their sin for more than twenty years, and when Jacob died, they fell before Joseph in fear of revenge. Guilt locks us in the prison of the past and makes us doubt even the joy we are given. Time cannot open that door. Only forgiveness can.

Forgiveness is not only something we receive; it is something we give. Those who cannot forgive keep saying “if only” and look for someone to blame. Those who forgive look for God’s purpose instead. Joseph named his sons Manasseh (“God has made me forget all my hardship”) and Ephraim (“God has made me fruitful”). He clearly remembered the day his brothers sold him, but that wound no longer ruled his life. His secret was one sentence: “You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good.” Just as Paul shook the viper off into the fire, we can shake off our past wounds and regrets before the Lord. It is not the wound itself but how we interpret it that either destroys us or protects us. Even the today we cannot understand, God will surely turn for good.

“As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring it about that many people should be kept alive, as they are today.”
Genesis 50:20
3Observation Questions
  1. Joseph had already welcomed his brothers, so why were they afraid again after their father died? What does this show us about guilt? Genesis 50:15–18
  2. What confession did Joseph put into the names of his two sons?
    He still vividly remembered what his brothers did, so what did he mean by “God has made me forget”? Genesis 41:51–52; 50:20
  3. How does Joseph speak of both his brothers’ evil intent and God’s providence? How did that perspective change the way he treated them? Genesis 45:5; 50:19–21
4Life Application
  1. Like the buried vase, is there an area of your life you are hiding, and does that secret end up controlling you? If you have ever confessed something to God and seen a stain become a sign of grace, share about it.
  2. What “if only…” do you keep repeating these days?
    If you added “But God…” after it, how would the sentence continue?
  3. Joseph kept no “blacklist.” Is there someone you still haven’t forgiven? This week, what is one small step you can take to shake that viper off into the fire?
5Prayer Together
  • That we would honestly confess our hidden sin and shame to the Father and be set free from slavery to sin
  • That we would be released from old guilt and fear, and fully enjoy today’s joy in the forgiveness of the cross
  • That we would lay down blame, forgive those who hurt us, and seek God’s purpose right where we are
  • That friends walking through a today they can’t understand would hold on to the faith of “But God meant it for good”
6Closing Prayer

Loving Father, there are stains in us we have never told anyone about. Like a broken vase buried in the ground, we have hidden our sin and become its slaves. Forgive us. In the love of the Father who embraces us and says, “You are worth more than the vase,” turn our stains of shame into evidence of Your grace. Let the lips that once said “if only” and blamed people and circumstances now become lips that seek Your will. Help us lay down the blacklists in our hearts and carry the confession of Manasseh and the hope of Ephraim. In the face of injustice, failure, and discouragement, let us hold fast to this confession: “But God meant it for good…” We believe the God who was with Joseph is with us today. In the name of Jesus Christ, who turns our past, our today, and our tomorrow for good, we pray.

Amen.
