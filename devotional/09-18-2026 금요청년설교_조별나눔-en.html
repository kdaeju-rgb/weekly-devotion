Small Group Discussion Sheet

The Upside-Down Law

Friday Young Adults Gathering · September 18, 2026
Text Genesis 26:26–33 (ESV) · Reference Luke 17:19 (ESV)
1Icebreaker

Share about a time you felt cheated or lost something unfairly. What did you feel in that moment?

2Message Summary

Trusting the God Who Gives the Well

The Philistines seized Isaac's wells again and again, yet Isaac never fought for them. Each time, he simply moved on and dug a new one. It wasn't weakness — Isaac trusted the God who gives the well more than the well itself. Every time he yielded, God provided another well, until at last, at Rehoboth ("room enough"), no one contended with him anymore.

In Isaac we glimpse Jesus Christ. On the cross, the One who held all power did nothing for himself — not because he was powerless, but because he endured for our sake. That is what real love looks like: choosing to become powerless because you truly love. The power that actually wins and overcomes is not money or influence — it is love alone.

"Isaac said to them, 'Why have you come to me, seeing that you hate me and have sent me away from you?'"
Genesis 26:27 · ESV
3Observation Questions
  1. When the Philistines seized Isaac's wells, he gave way instead of fighting for them. Why? Genesis 26:26–33
  2. Why did Jesus "do nothing" for himself on the cross — was it powerlessness, or something else?
  3. In Luke 17, what had the man done to whom Jesus said, "your faith has made you well"? Luke 17:19, ESV
4Life Application
  1. Think of a recent moment when you were treated unfairly. Did you want to fight back? How did you actually respond?
  2. The sermon named small daily practices — keeping your word, greeting a stranger, being kind even to people who make you uncomfortable. Which one could you try this week?
  3. Right now, are you trusting "the well" (what you have) more, or "the God who gives the well"? Share a specific situation.
5Prayer Together
  • For forgiveness where we have wanted to repay hurt with our own strength
  • For grace to live captured by the Lord's love, even in our weakness
  • For strength to practice small obedience this week — keeping our word, honoring others, showing kindness
  • For wells of living water to break open for the thirsty, wherever we go
6Closing Prayer

Loving Father God, thank you for teaching us through Isaac's life to trust you, the giver of the well, more than the well itself. We confess that we too have had moments when our wells were taken and stopped up. Forgive us for the times we wanted to repay unfairness and anger with our own strength. Lord Jesus, who accomplished everything on the cross by doing nothing for yourself — we recognize again today that this powerlessness was your greatest, most sovereign love for us. Thank you for enduring the cross for our sake. Lord, make us people who no longer build up strength to take revenge, but who live captured by your love even in our weakness. Let us begin with small obedience — not treating others carelessly, keeping our promises, reaching out first to strangers, and being kind even to those who make us uncomfortable. Let wells break open wherever we go, washing away people's thirst and pain, and let us always remember that you alone are the source of every well — young adults who live in constant gratitude. May our gratitude itself become our confession of faith in you. In the name of Jesus we pray.

Amen.
