DAY 01 · The Question Is the Sign

The Very Question
Is Already
an Answer.

If you are earnestly asking what God's will is, that itself is evidence you are in the middle of hardship.

"Your way was through the sea, your path through the great waters; yet your footprints were unseen."

Psalm 77:19 · ESV

DAY 01 · Meditation

No one ever asks,
"Why is everything going so well?"

In ministry, people almost always ask about God's will in the same kinds of moments — when prayers go unanswered for years, when suffering makes no sense, when faithful obedience brings no visible change.

One pastor recalls how his own uncertain, undecided years — the ones that felt like wasted time — turned out to be exactly the path that led him to where he stands today. What we cannot see now, we often see clearly only in hindsight.

DAY 01 · Reflect + Pray

Name the question you are carrying right now.

01 What is the question you are most earnestly asking God these days?
02 If that very question is a sign of hardship, what does it reveal about where you are right now?

Lord,
even in this season I cannot understand,
help me trust that your purpose is at work. Let me one day look back and call it grace.

— Amen.
DAY 02 · A Closed Door Is Guidance

Even a Closed Door
Can Be
the Spirit's Leading.

It was not opposition, not disaster. It was the Holy Spirit who closed the door.

"And they went through the region of Phrygia and Galatia, having been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia... the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them."

Acts 16:6–7 · ESV

DAY 02 · Meditation

The hardest discernment comes
when a clearly good plan is blocked.

Paul had prepared everything to reach Asia through Ephesus, with Timothy and Silas at his side. It was a strong, sensible, well-resourced plan. And the Spirit shut the door.

Discerning between an obviously right path and an obviously wrong one is rarely difficult. The real struggle comes when something good for God's kingdom is blocked anyway. Paul's team wandered, uncertain which way to turn next.

DAY 02 · Reflect + Pray

Recall a plan that was good — and still got blocked.

01 Have you ever had a plan you were sure was good for God's kingdom, only to see the door close?
02 How did you understand that closed door at the time — and how do you see it now?

Lord,
when my plans are blocked,
teach me to seek your Spirit before I complain. Let me trust there is grace behind every closed door.

— Amen.
DAY 03 · Too Great to Contain

Meeting a God
Who Cannot Be
Boxed In.

The moment I demand God answer prayer exactly as I expect, I have already shrunk him down to my own size.

"For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, declares the LORD."

Isaiah 55:8 · ESV

DAY 03 · Meditation

The Spirit's work
is not always miracles and success.

We assume that whenever the Spirit is at work, supernatural power and smooth success must follow. Yet in this passage, the Spirit blocked the path of faithful, well-prepared workers instead.

The danger in movements built on signs and spiritual experience is treating God like a well-solved equation — a God with no freedom left, tamed by our own expectations and logic. Accepting the God who exceeds our understanding is what real faith looks like.

DAY 03 · Reflect + Pray

Release the God you've kept inside your own box.

01 In what ways have I limited God to only working in the manner I expect?
02 How did I respond the last time God answered in a way I did not anticipate?

Lord,
forgive me for confining you
within the walls of my own understanding. Teach me to trust you even when you remain a mystery to me.

— Amen.
DAY 04 · Discerning Together

Not "I,"
but "We"
Discern Together.

Paul alone saw the vision, yet it was "we" who recognized it as the Lord's calling.

"A man of Macedonia was standing there, urging him and saying, 'Come over to Macedonia and help us.' ... concluding that God had called us to preach the gospel to them."

Acts 16:9–10 · ESV

DAY 04 · Meditation

Humility is remembering
you are part of the community before you lead it.

Paul was an apostle who had met the risen Christ himself, and the true leader of this journey. Yet he did not declare his vision as final truth and hand down a one-way decision to the others.

Instead, he brought it to Silas and Timothy and discerned it together. Authoritarianism is born the moment a leader forgets they are also a member of the community. Humble leadership discerns together, even with conviction in hand.

DAY 04 · Reflect + Pray

Recall a moment you decided alone, without asking anyone.

01 What decision have you recently pushed forward on your own conviction alone?
02 Who could you have brought into that discernment instead?

Lord,
the stronger my conviction,
make me all the more humble. Give me the wisdom to listen and discern together with others.

— Amen.
DAY 05 · Waiting on His Freedom

What Looks Small
Is Where the Story
Actually Begins.

In a Gentile city with not even a synagogue, at a riverside place of prayer, God began a work that would reshape a continent.

"and from there to Philippi, which is a leading city of the district of Macedonia and a Roman colony. We remained in this city some days."

Acts 16:12 · ESV

DAY 05 · Meditation

Ministry, mission, and life itself
do not depend on us.

The conversion of one merchant woman, Lydia, may have looked like a modest harvest to a team that had sailed across the sea to get there. Yet from that one unremarkable moment, God began the gospel movement that would transform an entire continent.

True faith is love, and love sets its object free. Releasing God from the boundaries of our expectations, and joyfully joining whatever he chooses to do — that is what it means to believe.

Closing Prayer

One Thing to Carry
from This Week's Devotion.

01 As the new semester begins, who around you is asking, "Come over and help us"?
02 This week, in what one area will you set God free from your own expectations?

Lord,
teach me to wait for your free work
rather than insist on my own plan. Let me hold onto the truth I already know and trust your faithful timing.

— Amen.
