Small Group Bible Study

A Path Not in Vain

Sunday Worship · October 25, 2026
Text Acts 23:11 (ESV) · See also Acts 23:1–22; 1 Corinthians 2:2; Mark 4:26–27
1Opening Up

Have you ever worked hard on something that didn’t turn out the way you hoped? If it felt like a failure at the time but later led to unexpected fruit, share that story.

2Reading the Passage

Take turns reading Acts 23:1–11 aloud, a few verses each. Then read verse 11 together once more and notice which word from the Lord to Paul stays with you.

  • 1–5Paul before the council and the high priest Ananias
  • 6–9The council divided over “the hope and the resurrection”
  • 10The tribune takes Paul into the barracks amid the uproar
  • 11That night the Lord stands by him: “Take courage”
3Message Summary

A path walked with the Lord is never in vain

Before he ever set foot in Korea, missionary Robert J. Thomas lost his wife and their unborn child. He was killed on the banks of the Taedong River without having preached a single real sermon. Yet the Bibles he threw out landed in the hands of Park Chun-gwon, the man who killed him, and of twelve-year-old Choi Chi-ryang. Park Yeong-sik papered his walls with the pages, and his house later became Jangdaehyun Church, the birthplace of the 1907 Pyongyang Revival. Paul, too, was living through “Is this really the right path?” days in Jerusalem. He had expected chains, but they came too soon, and he spent more time on trial than preaching. That night the risen Lord stood by him and said, “Take courage, for as you have testified to the facts about me in Jerusalem…” Paul thought he had accomplished nothing, but the Lord said he had already testified.

When we take stock of our lives, we must not judge them by what we have produced or by the world’s standards. Nor should we imagine that we accomplished anything on our own, because it is all God’s work. Martyn Lloyd-Jones’s simple gravestone bears only one verse: “For I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified.” Even an ordinary life is a life well lived if it holds stories of walking with God and nights of tearful prayer. In a Korean drama, a queen tells a mistreated servant, “I have already stepped into your life.” In the same way, the Lord has already stepped into ours and will never leave us alone. Even when we don’t know how, God makes the seed sprout and grow today.

“The following night the Lord stood by him and said, ‘Take courage, for as you have testified to the facts about me in Jerusalem, so you must testify also in Rome.’”
Acts 23:11
4Observation Questions
  1. What situation was Paul in just before verse 11? Instead of preaching the gospel in Jerusalem, what was he mostly doing? Acts 23:1–10
  2. How did the Lord come to Paul “the following night”? Paul had only been arrested and questioned, so what does the Lord’s verdict, “you have testified to the facts about me in Jerusalem,” tell us? Acts 23:11
  3. The very next day, more than forty men vowed to kill Paul. Did “Take courage” mean the hardship would disappear, or what was the Lord actually guaranteeing? Acts 23:12–22 · 20:22–24
5Life Application
  1. Is there a place in your life right now where you’re asking, “Is this really the right path?” If you’ve worked hard and the way seems blocked, share as much as you feel comfortable sharing.
  2. Have you ever measured your life by results or by the world’s standards and felt small?
    How might the Lord see the place where you feel sick, overwhelmed, or ordinary?
  3. In a season where nothing seems to change, what is one seed you can quietly sow this week? (a prayer for your family, a small act of service, a story of faith, etc.)
6Let's Pray Together
  • That those passing through a night of “Is this the right path?” would hear the Lord standing by them and saying, “Take courage”
  • That we would not count our lives by our results or the world’s scales, but trust the Lord who is accomplishing His work
  • That we would live as witnesses of Christ even where today looks sick or small
  • That seeds of God’s kingdom would keep growing through our church and our families, even when we don’t see it
7Closing Prayer

Loving Father, we remember how You did not let the blood of a young missionary on the Taedong River be wasted, and how You built Your church in that land through pages of Scripture pasted on a wall. Some of us are passing through a night of asking, “Is this the right path?” Lord, as You stood by Paul that night, stand by us, and let us hear You say, “Take courage.” Keep us from counting our lives by our results or weighing the Christian life on the world’s scales. Help us believe that it is not we but You who accomplish the work, and help us live as Your witnesses even where today looks small. Lord, You have already stepped into our lives and will never leave us alone. We believe that these faithful steps through days that seem unchanged are never in vain before You. In the name of Jesus Christ, who was crucified and rose again for us, we pray.

Amen.
Leader's Notes

The Surrounding Context (21:27–23:22)

Paul was seized in the temple and nearly beaten to death before the Roman tribune rescued him. He spoke from the barracks steps, but the crowd erupted at the words “to the Gentiles” (ch. 22). The next day the tribune brought him before the council to find out the facts (23:1–10). On the day after the Lord appeared to him “the following night” (v. 11), more than forty men vowed not to eat or drink until they had killed Paul (vv. 12–15). But Paul’s nephew heard of the plot, and Paul was moved under armed escort to Caesarea (vv. 16–35). The promise did not remove the hardship, but it shows God at work within it.

The Council Scene (23:1–9)

The high priest Ananias ordered Paul to be struck on the mouth (v. 2). Knowing the council was divided between Pharisees, who believed in the resurrection, and Sadducees, who denied it, Paul cried out, “It is with respect to the hope and the resurrection of the dead that I am on trial” (v. 6), and a great dissension broke out. At the heart of Paul’s defense was the risen Jesus. That is why the Lord calls this scene “testifying to the facts about me.”

Paul Knew Chains Were Coming (20:22–24; 21:10–14)

Before going to Jerusalem, Paul had already been told by the Spirit that “imprisonment and afflictions await me,” and he said, “I do not account my life of any value nor as precious to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus.” The point of connection in this sermon is that even someone fully prepared can face a night of asking, “Is this the right path?”

Robert Jermain Thomas (1839–1866)

A Welsh missionary whose wife, Caroline, died in Shanghai in 1864. In 1866 he sailed up the Taedong River to Pyongyang aboard the General Sherman and was killed there. The accounts of his Bibles passing to Park Chun-gwon, Choi Chi-ryang, and Park Yeong-sik, and of Park’s house becoming Neoldarigol Church and later Jangdaehyun Church, are testimonies handed down in the Korean church. Details vary by source, so keep the focus on the message that God did not let his life be in vain rather than on the historical specifics.

Facilitation Tips

  • Suggested time: Opening Up 10 min · Reading and Summary 15 min · Observation 15 min · Application 20 min · Prayer 15 min
  • For anyone facing illness, job loss, or grief, be careful that “Take courage” doesn’t sound like “just cheer up” or “you lack faith.” The sermon’s comfort is that the Lord stands beside us and keeps our path from being in vain, whatever the results.
  • If someone describes their life as small during Application 2, invite group members to each name one way they have seen that person “testify” through their life.
  • The “seed” in Application 3 doesn’t need to be big. It works well to briefly check in on each other’s seeds at the next meeting.
