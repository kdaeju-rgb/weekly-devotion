Small Group Discussion Sheet

My Identity

Friday Young Adults Gathering · October 30, 2026
Text Romans 1:1 (ESV) · Reference Philippians 2:6–7; 1:20 (ESV)
1Icebreaker

Growing up, was there something you owned that you really wanted to show off to your friends (trendy clothes, shoes, a bag, a game console)? Looking back now, how do you feel about it?

2Message Summary

Living well means living according to our purpose

The world says a good life means eating well and enjoying yourself, and social media is full of happy-looking photos. But no one shows the emptiness that comes after the camera turns off. Erich Fromm said we can never live well in the “having mode,” clinging to wealth, status, and power while fearing we will lose them. Just as Pastor Kim once showed off a pair of expensive Nix jeans in high school, we try to prove our worth through our school, job, salary, car, and clothes. But the value of anything comes from its purpose, and our purpose is decided by the God who made us. Living well means living a life led by God’s purpose.

In Romans 1:1, Paul introduces himself with three words. First, a servant. We are all bound to serve someone, but Paul gladly became a servant of Christ because he loved Him. “Become God’s servant, and you are set free; try to be free from God, and you become a slave.” Second, Paul. Saul, whose name means “asked for” or “to demand,” realized he was nothing before the Lord and chose to live as Paulos, “the small one.” Third, an apostle, one who is sent. No matter how expensive a speaker is, it can never be the message. There is no reason to be discouraged by poor specs or proud of great ones. A life in which Christ is honored in our bodies, whether by life or by death, is what it truly means to live well.

“Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle, set apart for the gospel of God.”
Romans 1:1
3Observation Questions
  1. What three words does Paul use to introduce himself? Why do you think he puts “servant” (literally, slave) first? Romans 1:1; Philippians 2:6–7
  2. What does the change from “Saul” (to demand) to “Paul” (small, to give up) mean?
    Why would Paul, with such an impressive background, call himself “the small one”? Romans 1:1; Acts 13:9
  3. An apostle is one who is sent. Where did Paul’s authority come from? What does the speaker illustration teach us about this? Romans 1:1
4Life Application
  1. These days, what do you use to prove your worth (school, grades, job, social media, appearance)? If you lost it, how would you describe who you are?
  2. “Become God’s servant, and you are set free; try to be free from God, and you become a slave.” What “master” is dragging you around right now? What would it look like in your life to serve the Lord gladly because you love Him?
  3. Have you ever felt discouraged or proud because of your “speaker specs”? This week, choose one concrete way to reveal Christ rather than yourself on campus or at work.
5Prayer Together
  • That we would stop trying to prove ourselves by what we have, and confess that our worth comes from God alone
  • That instead of being slaves to sin, desire, and others’ opinions, we would gladly serve the Lord out of love and enjoy true freedom
  • That we would lay down Saul’s ever-demanding heart, live as small ones, and be used by God to the very end
  • That we would live purpose-driven lives in which Christ is honored in our bodies, whether by life or by death
6Closing Prayer

Loving Father, thank You for setting us before Your Word again—we who wanted to live well but didn’t know what living well meant. We tried to fill our worth with better schools, better jobs, and better photos, but our hearts only grew emptier. We confess that the value of our lives comes not from what we have, but from You, the One who made us. Like Paul, we want to gladly become Your servants because we love You. Have mercy on us who, like Saul, have only ever demanded, and help us live as small ones—as Paulos. We know we are only speakers, not the message. Keep us from losing heart when we feel lacking and from exalting ourselves when we feel gifted, and let only Your gospel be seen through our lives. Whether by life or by death, may Christ be honored in our bodies, so that we may truly live well, without regret. In the name of Jesus Christ, who made us His children and calls us His friends, we pray.

Amen.
